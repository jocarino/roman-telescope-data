The idea

Each planet page shows one world. This page shows all of them at once — every modelled colour in the catalog, plotted together, so the patterns physics carves into the whole population become visible.

Honesty

Every colour here is a modelled prediction, not a photograph — the same swatches as the gallery, computed from each planet's reflected-light spectrum.

{# ── 2 · Temperature vs brightness scatter ─────────────────────────────── #}

Temperature × brightness

Each dot is a planet, drawn in its own modelled colour: left–right is how hot it is, up–down is how much of its star's light it reflects. The clusters are chemistry, not chance — cold gas giants keep their methane and reflect blue-green; temperate worlds wear bright water clouds; the hottest Jupiters are cloud-free and dark, tinted deep blue by light scattering off bare gas; orange dots are worlds around small red stars, tinted by their star's own light.

Every planet by temperature and reflectivity, coloured as itself. Hover to identify; click to visit.

{# ── 3 · What Roman keeps ──────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

How much colour survives Roman

The Roman Space Telescope's coronagraph sees visible light in only four bands, so it recovers an approximation of each planet's colour. ΔE ("delta-E") measures how far that approximation lands from the full-spectrum colour, in units of human perception: below ~2 the eye can't tell the difference, around 10 it reads as a clearly different shade, beyond 20 it's a different colour altogether.
