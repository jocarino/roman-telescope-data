## The pitches, as a recipient

**(a) links roundup — the only one I open.** The subject line is genuinely good: lowercase, concrete, and it collides two nouns no press release ever collides. And the best line in all three drafts is buried in it — *"which is worse and more honest"*. That's the sentence that makes me trust you.

**(c) unusual dataset — deleted at the subject line.** "a colour palette for every planet we know of" is vague where (a) is specific; "we know of" is a hedge, and "a colour palette" sounds like a Dribbble shot. Then "The interesting part is the honesty" tells me which part is interesting. That's my job. If you have to point at it, it isn't.

**(b) craft/process — deleted at the body.** "5,700 static pages, no framework" is a Show HN title, not a pitch; it's a stack brag and nobody forwards a stack. Then it does three fatal things in nine lines: "Hello," (you didn't look up my name), a press-kit link, and "Happy to write up the process if that's more useful" — which is you offering me homework. Keep it for Frontend Focus and Frontend Dogma only, where the stack genuinely is the story.

**The structural problem behind all three:** they are three templates mapped onto buckets of outlets, and the doc's fix — "personalise the first line" — is the most recognisable pattern in my inbox. A bespoke first sentence bolted to a template body reads *worse* than an honest form letter, because now you've shown me you know personalisation matters and you did the cheapest possible version. If the body could go to anyone, it goes to no one.

**The rewrite** (Web Curios, and this one is *only* for Web Curios):

> **Subject:** HD 189733b is cobalt blue and I can show my working
>
> Hi Matt,
>
> Nobody picked any of the colours on this site: https://…
>
> It takes all ~5,700 known exoplanets, models the light each one reflects, multiplies that by its own star, and runs the result through the CIE colour-matching functions — so you get the colour a human eye would see. HD 189733b comes out deep cobalt because sodium in its atmosphere eats the yellow. Then there's a slider for orbital phase, because planets change colour as they turn, and a toggle for what the Roman telescope will actually be able to see, which is much worse and much more honest.
>
> None of it is photographs and it says so on every page. Five solar-system planets are in there as a check, so you can see how close the model gets to Jupiter.
>
> Written by hand in the evenings, in Portugal, by me, with no AI writing this email.
>
> João

What changed and why: **one planet is named, with a physical reason.** That's the difference between a dataset and a sentence I can print. The press-kit link is gone — a links newsletter is one person on a laptop, and a "press kit" tells them a marketing function exists behind you. The "no framework" brag is gone; he doesn't care. "Screenshots" is gone; the site *is* the screenshot. The AI line is there because his contact page asks for it, which is the only kind of personalisation that counts — evidence you read the page.

**On subject lines generally:** the job isn't to describe the project, it's to be a sentence I'd forward. Ranked: "HD 189733b is cobalt blue and I can show my working" > "every known exoplanet now has a hex code" > the doc's (a) > (c) > (b). Never put a number *first* — "5,700 static pages" reads as a stat, and stats are what marketing emails open with.

## Gaps

- **No plan for form-only outlets.** Hover States, Sidebar, Web Tools Weekly, and every gallery are forms — no craft, no relationship, no burn risk. These should be one boring hour on a Tuesday, decoupled entirely from the email wave, and the doc treats them as if they were pitches.
- **The doc's own dependency is deferred.** It calls the phase-slider clip effectively mandatory for Design Spells and Hover States, but the clip exporter lives in [08-short-video.md](../08-short-video.md), which the hub marks **deferred**. Either the clip gets built or those two targets are pitched without motion. Say which.
- **Press kit mismatch.** [02-press-kit.md](../02-press-kit.md) lists no motion asset and no full-grid poster; doc 12 requires both. 02 also needs to say the licence is CC BY 4.0 *on the page*, because Hover States and Colossal will look for it. Otherwise the two docs agree well — the 15/40/150-word ladder in 02 is exactly right.
- **Hub contradiction.** The README puts 12 *after* Show HN in Phase 1; doc 12's rule 2 says pitch the four core outlets *before* Hacker News. One of them is wrong and a reader will follow whichever they read second. (Doc 12 is right: curators hate arriving third.)
- **No "what if it works" plan.** Two placements in a week is the good outcome and it's also the moment the site is judged. Nothing here says who watches the server that evening.

## Better approaches

1. **Send one email, to Web Curios, and wait.** He invites it in writing, he's alive, he reads it himself, and he's read by the other curators. One send, then a week of watching what actually happens on the site. Everything else in wave one is noise around this.
2. **Be findable instead of pitchable.** The three biggest names in the doc — Waxy, kottke, Tiny Awards — do not take pitches in any meaningful sense; they take *discoveries*, and Tiny Awards structurally requires someone else to nominate you. The move is a MetaFilter Projects post, a real Bluesky presence, and showing up at **HTML Day on 8 Aug**. That out-earns 80% of the target list and it's the half of the plan doc 12 doesn't have.
3. **Ship something that travels without you.** A curator features what they can host a picture of. The 3000px 5,700-swatch poster and the free .ase/Procreate packs get re-posted by people you never emailed. This is currently ranked last in the doc ("after the newsletter wave") and should be concurrent.
4. **Hold two targets for 30 August.** Roman launches in a month. That is the one week when "what will it actually see" is news and even design newsletters will take an exoplanet story. Spending kottke and It's Nice That now, cold, is spending them at their cheapest.
5. **Batch the forms, separately and joylessly.** Hover States, Sidebar, Web Tools Weekly, SiteInspire, Godly, Fonts In Use. One hour, no personalisation, no feelings.
6. **Don't pitch the aggregators at all.** Muzli, Web Designer News, Hacker Newsletter, Frontend Focus's scrapers — all downstream of 1–5. Pitching them costs an evening and changes nothing.

**When the first wave goes quiet** — and it will, because silence is the normal outcome — the wrong move is the one everybody makes: a second email. "Just bumping this", or worse, a v2 email about a new feature to the same person three weeks later. That's how you become a name someone filters. The right move is to make the second contact not an email: reply once, publicly, in the place the curator already is, when they post something your thing genuinely answers. The doc's "never follow up" rule is right and should stay — but it currently also bans the one legitimate second email, which is *news*: Roman launching is news, a scientist citing you is news, being featured somewhere is news. New fact, new email. Same link, never.

## The one thing I'd change

**Name one planet.** Every pitch here sells a dataset — 5,700, CIE, sRGB, no framework — and a dataset is not something I can print. "HD 189733b is cobalt blue because sodium eats the yellow" is a sentence with a number I can check and a picture attached, and it makes the reader want the other 5,699. The doc's rule that "the exoplanet part is the hook, never the pitch" is the mistake that produced pitches (b) and (c): it pushed the author to lead with mechanism and craft, which are the two things no reader has ever forwarded. The exoplanets *are* the pitch. There are a thousand palette generators and exactly one where the colour was decided by methane.
